Zephyr Bank: rebuilding trust in mobile banking

Zephyr Bank’s app had a 2.1-star rating and customers avoided it. I led the end-to-end redesign of its iOS, Android and online banking experience, focusing on the five tasks people do most, and on making every money movement feel safe.

Overview

Project overview

Zephyr Bank is a regional US bank with 850,000+ customers across the Midwest. Its mobile app had a 2.1-star rating and most customers avoided it, choosing branches and phone support instead. Digital-first banks like Chime and Simple were winning younger customers fast.

Working with Harman Connected Services, I led the end-to-end redesign of the iOS and Android apps and the companion online banking site, from research to a cross-platform design system.

The challenge

Problem statement and pain points

The app had grown feature by feature for eight years with no shared design. Core tasks such as depositing a check or sending money took up to 9 taps, crashed often, and gave no feedback. Customers didn’t trust it with their money.

Problem statement

Zephyr customers can’t complete everyday banking tasks on their phones with confidence. A slow, confusing and unreliable app pushes them to branches and call centres, raising Zephyr’s costs and sending younger customers to digital-first competitors.

How might we

How might we turn an app customers avoid into one they trust with their money, so everyday banking takes seconds, not a branch visit?

Approach

My approach

  1. Research first: interviews, app-review mining, analytics and a competitor audit before any design.
  2. Fix the top five tasks: balance, deposit, send money, pay bills and card controls cover 90% of sessions.
  3. Trust by design: biometrics, clear status and plain-language security messages throughout.
  4. Test with real money anxieties: moderated sessions with customers’ own scenarios, then A/B tests in production.
  5. One system, two platforms: an 80+ component library shared by iOS, Android and web.

Test

A/B testing onboarding

We tested three onboarding approaches with 9,000 new users over four weeks.

34%Variant A · 5-screen tutorial
52%Variant B · skip + tooltips
78%Variant C · progressive disclosure (winner)

Progressive disclosure won by introducing features in context, the first time a customer needed them, instead of front-loading a tutorial.

Principles

Design laws applied

ReachabilityFitts’s Law

Applied: Primary actions sit in the bottom third of the screen, within thumb reach.

Impact: Faster one-handed use on the go.

Fewer choicesHick’s Law

Applied: Home shows four quick actions instead of a 14-item menu.

Impact: Users reach key tasks in one tap.

Familiar patternsJakob’s Law

Applied: Bottom tab bar and card layouts that match leading banking apps.

Impact: No learning curve for switchers.

Memorable momentsPeak–End Rule

Applied: Clear success screens after deposits and transfers.

Impact: Customers finish tasks feeling confident.

SpeedDoherty Threshold

Applied: Skeleton loading and optimistic UI keep responses under 400ms.

Impact: The app feels instant even on slow networks.

SecurityTrust cues

Applied: Masked numbers, biometric prompts and plain-language security notes.

Impact: Higher confidence with money tasks.

Accessibility

Built for every customer

Readable

  • Supports iOS Dynamic Type and Android font scaling
  • Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1
  • Amounts never truncated

Operable

  • 48px minimum touch targets
  • VoiceOver and TalkBack labels
  • No time-outs during deposits

Understandable

  • Plain-language errors with next steps
  • Status shown by icon and text
  • Consistent placement of actions

Deliver

Design system & handoff

  1. 80+ components shared across iOS, Android and web, with platform-specific variants where conventions differ.
  2. Tokens for colour, type, spacing and elevation, synced to code.
  3. Annotated specs in Figma with states, edge cases and accessibility notes.
  4. Weekly design QA with developers during each sprint.

Closing

Key takeaways

  • Trust is everything in FinTech. Biometric login was the single most impactful feature.
  • Test with real money anxieties. Lab tests missed the stress of “is my money safe?”
  • Progressive disclosure beats tutorials. Customers learn by doing.
  • Accessibility expands the market. Larger targets and contrast helped everyone.
