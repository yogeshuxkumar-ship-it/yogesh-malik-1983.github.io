Redesigning Axelos.com with Kentico CMS, .NET and Contentstack

Axelos, the global home of ITIL® and PRINCE2® certifications, needed a complete digital transformation. Its website served users in more than 200 countries but had serious usability and accessibility gaps. I led the UX/UI redesign, from research and information architecture to a WCAG 2.1 AA design system.

Overview

Project overview

Axelos owns the world’s most widely used best-practice frameworks, including ITIL®, PRINCE2®, MSP® and AgileSHIFT®. The site is where professionals, organisations and training partners learn about these certifications, compare them and find a way to get certified.

The old site packed in too much content, buried product value and offered no clear path to enrolment, all while needing to meet strict UK Government accessibility standards.

What had to change
  • Dense, poorly organised content and confusing navigation
  • Product value and benefits not clearly communicated
  • No clear subscription or onboarding path
  • Accessibility gaps against WCAG 2.1 AA

The challenge

Problem statement and pain points

  1. Information overload and poor UX: excessive, badly organised content and confusing navigation made it hard to find information or complete key actions.
  2. Weak product focus and engagement: features and benefits weren’t clear, and there was no streamlined subscription or onboarding flow, which hurt acquisition and retention.
  3. Inefficient journey to conversion: no intuitive path to subscribing or buying, so users abandoned tasks because of friction and ambiguity.

Approach

My approach and goals

  1. Accessibility-first design: ran a WCAG 2.1 AA audit and rebuilt the component library with accessibility built in from day one.
  2. Content strategy overhaul: worked with the content team to create clear certification journey maps.
  3. Search UX redesign: redesigned filtering and directory search, cutting time-to-result by 60%.
  4. Mobile-first responsive system: adaptive layouts tested across 12 device configurations.

Stakeholders

Navigating government requirements

UK Government stakeholders, as co-owners of Axelos, required strict accessibility compliance. Marketing wanted visually rich, animation-heavy pages.

I proposed progressive enhancement: every core function fully accessible, with optional animation that respects reduced-motion preferences. Both sides approved, and the site passed WCAG AA on its first audit.

Research

Key research findings

Interviews, open card sorting and task analysis showed that the site’s structure reflected internal taxonomy, not how users think.

Users struggled to find content

Navigation categories reflected internal taxonomy, so users hesitated and explored several sections before finding anything.

Design impact: restructured the IA around users’ mental models, which led directly to card sorting.

Users think in goals, not platform features

Participants grouped content by what they wanted to do (learn, apply, download) rather than by CMS feature.

Design impact: navigation and content grouped around user goals, with less internal jargon.

Shallow navigation and strong filters win

Users avoided deep hierarchies and finished tasks faster when filters were available early.

Design impact: reduced category depth and promoted high-value filters.

Inconsistent terms reduced confidence

Different names for the same thing caused second-guessing and rechecking.

Design impact: standardised terms across navigation, content and filters.

Recommendations

UX recommendations implemented

Recommendations were prioritised by impact on conversion and accessibility, and each came with an expected impact we could measure.

Priority 1 · Critical improvements

Redesign homepage with a clear value proposition

Issue: Users don’t immediately understand what Axelos does.

  • “Why Axelos?” hero message
  • Role-based quick links (IT professional, PM, executive)
  • Clear differentiation from competitors
  • Social proof: logos and statistics
Expected impact: +30% click-through

Interactive certification roadmap

Issue: Certification pathways are confusing and hard to visualise.

  • Visual ITIL / PRINCE2 / MSP progression
  • Time, cost and difficulty for each level
  • Recommendations based on current role
  • “Start here” on every pathway
Expected impact: +40% clarity, +25% conversions

Transparent pricing & comparison tool

Issue: Pricing is unclear and hard to compare.

  • Clear pricing table per course option
  • Self-study vs instructor-led vs online
  • Visible financing and payment plans
  • Group discount calculator
Expected impact: +35% confidence, −20% abandonment

Priority 2 · Feature enhancements

Simplify enrolment

Current state: Multi-step form process.

  • One-page or minimal-step checkout
  • Progress indicator
  • Guest checkout option
  • Social login (Google, Microsoft)

Post-purchase onboarding

Current state: Unclear next steps after enrolment.

  • Welcome email with clear next steps
  • Learning-platform orientation tour
  • First-module walkthrough
  • Success milestones

Learning community

Current state: Limited community engagement.

  • Forums by course level
  • Study-group matching
  • User-generated study tips
  • Badges for motivation

Priority 3 · Design polish & accessibility

Mobile-first responsive design

Current state: Optimise for mobile users on the go.

  • Mobile-optimised layouts
  • Touch-friendly buttons and navigation
  • Fast loading times

WCAG 2.1 AA compliance

Current state: Accessible to everyone, including people with disabilities.

  • Colour contrast ratios
  • Keyboard navigation
  • Screen-reader compatibility
  • Captions for video content

Principles

Design laws applied

Proven UX laws guided decisions where research alone didn’t settle the choice.

Familiar patternsJakob’s Law

Applied: Navigation, page layouts and interactions follow established content-platform conventions.

Impact: Shorter learning curve, more confidence.

Managing complexityTesler’s Law

Applied: Smart defaults and filters move complexity from users into the system.

Impact: Powerful functionality without overwhelming users.

Interaction efficiencyFitts’s Law

Applied: High-frequency actions and key CTAs made larger and placed consistently.

Impact: Faster tasks, fewer interaction errors.

Decision overloadHick’s Law

Applied: Fewer visible options, grouped content and progressive disclosure.

Impact: Faster orientation, fewer abandoned paths.

Cognitive loadMiller’s Law

Applied: Content chunked into meaningful groups with simpler hierarchies.

Impact: Better comprehension, smoother navigation.

Trust through clarityAesthetic–Usability Effect

Applied: A clean, structured, distraction-free UI with consistent type and spacing.

Impact: Higher perceived usability and professionalism.

Closing

Key takeaways

  • User-centred clarity drives engagement: a restructured IA and simpler navigation helped every audience find content faster and act with confidence.
  • Design systems bring consistency and scale: a modular, WCAG-compliant system sped up handoff and supports multilingual and partner pages.
  • Storytelling shows product value: visual pathways and real use cases made ITIL®, PRINCE2® and MSP® easier to tell apart.
  • Conversion-focused UX pays off: simpler CTAs and form flows lifted registrations and cut drop-off.
  • Collaborative governance builds trust: regular workshops and transparent documentation kept every team aligned.
  • A modern content stack enables agility: Contentstack let editors manage multilingual content, SEO and updates without developers.
