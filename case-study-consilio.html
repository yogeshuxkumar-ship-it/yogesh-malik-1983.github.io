Consilio eDiscovery: moving legal review from desktop to the web

At Harman Connected Services I led UX for migrating Consilio’s eDiscovery suite from a legacy desktop application to a modern web platform used by 5,000+ legal professionals, redesigning every workflow from legal hold to final production.

Overview

Project overview

eDiscovery is how legal teams find, preserve, review and hand over electronic evidence (email, chat, files) for litigation and investigations. Mistakes are expensive: a missed document or a wrongly produced privileged email can change the outcome of a case.

Consilio’s suite ran as a legacy desktop application. Moving it to the web meant redesigning workflows for thousands of reviewers, case managers and litigation-support staff without slowing down live matters.

Before: legacy desktop
  • Installed per machine, hard to update
  • Workflows built around the database, not the legal process
  • Little validation, so errors surfaced late
  • Each module looked and behaved differently
→
After: modern web platform
  • Browser-based, one workspace per matter
  • Workflows mirror real eDiscovery stages
  • Validation and guidance at every risky step
  • One pattern library across 8 modules

The challenge

Problem statement and pain points

  1. High-stakes errors: coding or production mistakes carried legal risk and were costly to fix.
  2. Slow processing: reviewers lost time switching between disconnected screens and dialogs.
  3. Heavy support load: unclear instructions and missing validation drove a steady stream of support requests.
  4. Inconsistent modules: eight modules each had their own patterns, so every new area meant relearning the tool.

Approach

My approach

  1. Stakeholder interviews and requirements gathering with legal, litigation-support and product teams.
  2. Workflow analysis: observing how reviewers and case managers actually move through a matter.
  3. Information architecture aligned with real-world eDiscovery stages, from legal hold to production.
  4. Validation and instructional design at every step where an error would be costly.
  5. Reusable design patterns shared across all 8 modules of the suite.

Research

What we learned

Users think in legal stages, not database tables

Reviewers and case managers described their work as hold, collect, review and produce, not by the tool’s internal objects.

Design impact: navigation rebuilt around the eDiscovery lifecycle.

Context is everything in review

Reviewers needed the document, its family and the coding options at the same time; switching windows broke concentration.

Design impact: a three-pane review workspace.

Errors came from unclear, unvalidated steps

Most costly mistakes happened in setup-heavy tasks like production, where settings were scattered and nothing was checked.

Design impact: guided wizards with validation and clear instructions.

Every action must be defensible

Teams had to show who did what and when, months later, sometimes in court.

Design impact: explicit status, audit trails and package manifests.

Principles

Design principles applied

Where research left a choice open, these principles decided it.

Legal-grade trustDefensibility first

Applied: Every action keeps who, what and when; status is always explicit and auditable.

Impact: Teams can defend their process in court.

Human in the loopAI is advisory

Applied: Model suggestions show confidence and reasons, and need reviewer confirmation.

Impact: Speed without giving up legal judgement.

Familiar patternsJakob’s Law

Applied: Desktop conventions reviewers relied on (panes, shortcuts, queues) were kept on the web.

Impact: A shorter learning curve during migration.

Cognitive loadProgressive disclosure

Applied: Complex settings sit inside guided steps, like the production wizard.

Impact: Fewer configuration errors.

One pattern libraryConsistency

Applied: Tables, chips, headers and panels shared across all 8 modules.

Impact: Learn once, use everywhere.

ValidationError prevention

Applied: Inline validation and clear instructions at risky steps such as production.

Impact: 40% fewer user errors.

Closing

Key takeaways

  • Mirror the real process: when navigation follows the legal lifecycle, users always know where they are.
  • Prevent errors where they cost the most: validation and instructions at production and coding steps paid off more than anywhere else.
  • Migration is a chance to unify: one pattern library turned 8 inconsistent modules into one product.
  • Trust is a design output: explicit status and audit history make legal teams confident in the tool.
