As Lead Experience Designer at AbsenceSoft, I mapped and redesigned the end-to-end journeys of a multi-tenant FMLA, absence and accommodation platform used by 10,000+ employees, so employees, HR, managers and admins always know what happens next.

Overview

Project overview

When an employee needs time away from work, a change at work, or both, the request touches HR, a people manager, payroll, timekeeping and policy teams. For the employee it often happens during a difficult moment, and internal handoffs can feel like a maze.

AbsenceSoft manages FMLA, absence and workplace accommodation cases for employers. My job was to turn fragmented, form-heavy workflows into one coordinated, understandable journey, from the first question to a supported return.

Before
  • Leave and accommodation handled as separate, disconnected processes
  • Employees repeat information and can’t tell what happens next
  • Case owners track deadlines across inboxes and spreadsheets
  • Sensitive details visible to more people than necessary
→
After
  • One linked case timeline across leave and accommodation
  • Plain-language intake with clear next steps
  • Every task has an owner and a due date
  • Role-based access: managers see only what they need

The challenge

Problem statement and pain points

  1. Lost in the process: employees repeated information and couldn’t see what would happen next.
  2. Missed deadlines: FMLA and accommodation steps have legal timelines, but tasks were scattered across tools.
  3. Privacy risk: managers needed to plan coverage without seeing health information.
  4. Heavy training and support: complex screens meant long onboarding for HR teams and frequent support tickets.

How might we make the next action obvious, keep the conversation supportive and the record dependable, without exposing more than a role needs?

Approach

My approach

  1. Journey mapping for every role across the full leave and accommodation lifecycle.
  2. Workflow analysis with leave specialists to find where cases stall and where rework happens.
  3. Wireframes and high-fidelity prototypes for employee intake, case management and the interactive process.
  4. Embedded in agile sprints with engineering, reaching 95% design-to-development accuracy.
  5. Iterative releases validated with users, lifting satisfaction from 3.2 to 4.5 out of 5.

Principles

Design principles applied

Six principles that resolved the tension between standard steps and individual circumstances.

Need to knowPrivacy by role

Applied: Each role sees only what it needs; managers never see health details.

Impact: Trust from employees and compliance teams.

ClarityNext action, owner, date

Applied: Every screen says who acts next, why and by when.

Impact: Fewer status-chasing contacts.

Cognitive loadProgressive disclosure

Applied: Intake asks intent first and shows only relevant fields.

Impact: Higher completion, less rework.

Human judgementAutomation assists

Applied: Rules create tasks and reminders; qualified staff make the decisions.

Impact: Speed without automated case decisions.

Error preventionVisible exceptions

Applied: Missing info, date changes, overdue tasks and sync errors get an owner.

Impact: Nothing fails silently.

Familiar patternsJakob’s Law

Applied: Standard queues, steppers and case records that HR teams already know.

Impact: 30% less training time.

Closing

Key takeaways

  • Design for the hardest moment: plain language and clear next steps matter most when someone is unwell or stressed.
  • Privacy is an interaction pattern: role-based views let managers help without seeing what they shouldn’t.
  • Ownership beats reminders: giving every task an owner and a date did more than any notification.
  • Sit with engineering: being in the sprints is how 95% design-to-development accuracy happened.
