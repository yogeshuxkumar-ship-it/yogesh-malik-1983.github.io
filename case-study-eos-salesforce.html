Evolution of Sales: designing a B2B Salesforce storefront for Capita

Capita’s sales process relied on manual data entry and inconsistent documents, which slowed order fulfilment. I led the design of a Salesforce B2B Commerce storefront that lets business buyers find, configure and order services on their own.

Overview

Project overview

Capita’s sales processes were manual, which delayed order fulfilment and created operational inefficiency. There were no standard templates for key documents such as proposals and SLAs across departments, which made the workflow even harder.

The goal was a self-service storefront where Capita business buyers and procurement teams could browse entitled services, see custom pricing and place orders without waiting on a sales rep.

The challenge

Problem statement and pain points

  1. Manual data entry caused 40% of order-processing delays.
  2. No standard templates for proposals and SLAs across departments.
  3. Sales reps spent 60% of their time on admin tasks.
  4. High support-ticket volume caused by confusing interface patterns.

Approach

My approach

A structured, user-centred process focused on fast validation and stakeholder alignment.

  1. Discovery and research: 8 stakeholder interviews and 8 user observation sessions to map pain points.
  2. Information architecture: navigation restructured around task frequency, cutting clicks-to-action by 50%.
  3. Wireframing and prototyping: high-fidelity Figma prototypes tested with 15 end users.
  4. Design system: a modular component library, consistent across 40+ screens.
  5. Handoff and build support: worked alongside developers for pixel-accurate implementation.

Stakeholders

Stakeholder and client engagement

I presented progress every two weeks to the Director of Digital Commerce and the VP of Sales. Every presentation was framed around business KPIs (cart completion rate, average order processing time and support-ticket volume) instead of design terms.

Stakeholders asked for 12 extra fields on the order form. A card-sorting workshop and abandonment data aligned the team on 4 essential fields, and form completion rose by 35%.

Audience

Target audience

Capita business buyers and procurement professionals in specific Lines of Business (LOBs). They expect a consumer-grade experience, but their organisations have structured purchasing: entitlement-based product visibility, custom pricing and account-level order history.

  1. From experienced buyers who know exactly which SKUs they need, to first-time users exploring the catalogue.
  2. Bulk ordering, quote generation and integration with back-end systems such as RCA.
  3. Branding and UI customisation to reflect each LOB and build trust.

Team leadership

  3. Process ownership: final calls on component library architecture and interaction patterns.
  4. Cross-functional alignment: daily syncs with engineering to clear blockers early.

Empathise

Research and discovery

I ran 8 contextual inquiry sessions with B2B buyers across 3 customer segments:

  1. Procurement managers prioritised quick reordering and order tracking.
  2. Field sales reps needed mobile-friendly catalogue browsing that works offline.
  3. Finance teams needed invoice downloads and payment history in self-service.

Key questions

How easy is it to find what you need, and is product information and pricing clear?
What challenges do you face when placing orders, and how do you work with account managers on complex purchases?
Do you feel confident sharing sensitive business information, and what do you think of the security features?

Principles

Design laws applied

Proven UX laws guided decisions where research alone didn’t settle the choice.

Decision complexityHick’s Law

Applied: Reduced category depth using card-sorting insights, and put advanced filters behind progressive disclosure.

Impact: Faster product discovery and less hesitation.

Cognitive loadMiller’s Law

Applied: Grouped specifications into clusters (performance, compatibility, pricing) and broke checkout into smaller steps.

Impact: Better understanding of technical data, fewer checkout errors.

Interaction efficiencyFitts’s Law

Applied: Enlarged primary CTAs (Add to cart, Reorder, Checkout) and placed frequent actions in the same spot on every screen.

Impact: Quicker tasks, especially for repeat and bulk orders.

FamiliarityJakob’s Law

Applied: Followed familiar B2B eCommerce patterns (PLP → PDP → Cart → Checkout) and Salesforce conventions.

Impact: Shorter learning curve, faster onboarding.

Gestalt principleLaw of Proximity

Applied: Grouped pricing, availability and delivery info, and clustered filters with technical specs.

Impact: Clearer information, less visual noise.

Perceived easeAesthetic–Usability Effect

Applied: Consistent spacing, type and components, balancing dense data with white space.

Impact: More user confidence, lower perceived complexity.

Accessibility

WCAG 2.1 AA, aligned with UK GDS

Accessibility was designed and validated against UK Government Digital Service guidelines and WCAG 2.1 AA, so the platform stays usable and compliant through long, task-heavy workflows.

PerceivableWCAG 1.x · make content perceivable

  • Contrast ≥ 4.5:1 for text
  • No colour-only indicators
  • Readable at 200% text zoom

Readable for users with visual impairments.

OperableWCAG 2.x · make it operable

  • Full keyboard navigation
  • Logical tab order
  • Visible focus states

Users can complete every task without a mouse.

UnderstandableWCAG 3.x · make it understandable

  • Plain, consistent language
  • Clear form labels and instructions
  • Descriptive error messages

Fewer errors in complex B2B workflows.

RobustWCAG 4.1 · make it robust

  • Semantic component structure
  • Accessible forms and alerts
  • No fragile custom interactions

Works reliably with assistive technologies.

Enterprise dataWCAG 1.3, 1.4 · GDS patterns

  • Proper table structures
  • Icons paired with labels
  • Clear spacing for dense data

Dense data stays scannable and unambiguous.

Feedback & errorsWCAG 3.3, 4.1

  • Inline validation
  • Clear success and error states
  • Status messages announced properly

Users always know the system state and next step.

Solution

Design solution

  1. Smart search: faceted search with predictive suggestions and recent searches for returning buyers.
  2. Streamlined checkout: 5 steps reduced to 3, with a persistent order summary and inline validation.
  3. Order dashboard: status, tracking, invoices and quick reorder in one place, reducing support calls.
  4. Bulk order table: spreadsheet-style bulk ordering with CSV upload.
  5. Mobile-first catalogue: product grid and filters built for touch, for field sales reps.

  • Connected systems: integration with CRM, billing and scheduling tools.
