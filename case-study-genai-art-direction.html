Overview

Project overview

Capita’s marketing teams wanted to use generative AI tools like Midjourney and DALL·E to create campaign imagery, but results were inconsistent, risky and slow. I designed an end-to-end art direction system, covering style framework, prompt templates, workflow, review checklist and a lightweight studio tool, so anyone could produce brand-consistent imagery in minutes.

The challenge

Problem statement and pain points

Interest in AI imagery was high, but there were no shared rules. Twelve marketers were each prompting in their own way, producing images that looked like twelve different brands, while the brand team had no way to review them objectively.

Problem statement

Capita marketers can’t reliably create on-brand AI imagery. Without shared styles, prompts or review criteria, each person gets different, unpredictable results, spends hours iterating, and still risks publishing images that are off-brand or legally unclear.

How might we

How might we let anyone on the marketing team create professional, brand-consistent AI imagery in minutes, without becoming a prompt engineer?

Approach

My approach

  1. Audit what exists: collected and scored 400 AI images the team had already made.
  2. Define on-brand: turned brand guidelines into measurable visual rules.
  3. Systemise prompts: modular templates with variables instead of free text.
  4. Design the workflow: clear steps, owners and quality gates from brief to approval.
  5. Build and test the tool: a simple studio, validated with 12 marketers over 3 rounds.

Define

Visual style framework

Four style directions turned Capita’s brand personality into concrete visual rules. Each one defines mood, palette, lighting, composition, where to use it and what to avoid, so presets give predictable results.

Rules shared by every style
  • Capita blue appears as an accent, never as a colour cast
  • People reflect UK workforce diversity in age, ethnicity and ability
  • No readable text, logos or brand marks inside AI images
  • Hands, faces and screens checked closely for artefacts
  • Always 16:9, 1:1 or 4:5 crops from the same master image
  • Every image labelled “AI-generated” in the asset library

Governance

Responsible AI guardrails

  1. Approved tools only: enterprise licences with commercial usage rights.
  2. Labelling: every image tagged “AI-generated” with its prompt and date in the library.
  3. No real people or brands: no likenesses, logos or trademarks in prompts.
  4. Human sign-off: nothing is published without a brand reviewer’s approval.

Principles

Design laws applied

Usability heuristicRecognition over recall

Applied: Style presets and dropdowns replace writing prompts from memory.

Impact: Anyone can produce a strong prompt first time.

Fewer choicesHick’s Law

Applied: Four style directions instead of open-ended styling.

Impact: Faster decisions and consistent output.

ComplexityProgressive disclosure

Applied: Advanced parameters hidden until needed.

Impact: Beginners aren’t overwhelmed; experts keep control.

Visibility of statusFeedback loops

Applied: Every result shows a live brand score.

Impact: Users learn what “on-brand” means by seeing it.

Closing

Key takeaways

  • Systems beat skills. Good presets let non-experts outperform ad-hoc expert prompting.
  • Measure “on-brand”. A scorecard turned opinion into shared language.
  • Guardrails build confidence. Clear legal and labelling rules made people willing to use AI.
  • Design for the reviewer too. Approval speed mattered as much as generation speed.
