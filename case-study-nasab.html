Nasab: a private digital home for every family

End-to-end product and visual design for a bilingual, Arabic-first app that gives each extended family its own verified family tree, shared family fund, wedding-gift campaigns, voting, events and digital cards. The aim was a product that feels warm, crafted and dignified, not like a spreadsheet.

Summary

The 30-second version

Problem

Extended Gulf families run their tree, shared fund and wedding gifts across WhatsApp groups, spreadsheets and cash envelopes.

Approach

One private, verified space per family, designed Arabic-first, with privacy, dignity and money transparency built in.

Outcome

A distinctive "Weave" identity, a bilingual design system, and five core screens designed in both Arabic (RTL) and English (LTR).

Context

Families are big, close-knit and run on goodwill

In Saudi Arabia and the wider Gulf, a family name (nasab, lineage) can connect hundreds of relatives across seven or more generations. Families often keep a shared fund (sandooq) for emergencies and occasions, and relatives traditionally pool money for a member's wedding.

Today this runs on a patchwork: a family elder's hand-drawn tree, a treasurer's spreadsheet, and dozens of WhatsApp groups. Contributions are hard to track, privacy is all-or-nothing, and nothing feels worthy of something as personal as family.

How might we give an extended family one private, trustworthy home for its lineage, money and occasions, and make it feel as warm as a family gathering?

Design principles

What guided every decision

01

Dignity over gamification

No streaks, confetti or badges. Elders who have passed away are honoured with a gold medallion and "in memory" label.

02

Privacy by default

Members can be fully private or "name only". Women's details in particular are never exposed without consent.

03

Money must be transparent

Fees are always shown, the net amount is stated, and every figure uses tabular numerals.

04

Arabic first, not translated

Layouts were designed RTL-first, then mirrored to English, so neither version feels secondary.

Visual identity

The "Weave" identity

The brand mark is the Arabic letter ن (nūn) in a gold medallion. Backgrounds use a subtle woven lattice that draws on Sadu textile patterns, a nod to the threads that tie a family together.

Key screens

Five screens that carry the product

Each screen was designed in Arabic first and then mirrored to English.

العربية · RTL
English · LTR
01

Interactive family tree

شجرة العائلة

Hundreds of relatives have to stay readable at any zoom level. Branches collapse to a count, a deceased elder gets a gold medallion, and privacy states apply everywhere in the tree.

  • Instant search to find any relative across seven generations
  • Legend chips for deceased, spouse, divorced and private, so relationships are clear without reading labels
  • Collapsible branches that show a member count (+9, +5) to keep the tree navigable
  • "You" anchor and a persistent profile bar, so the user always knows where they are
  • Privacy states such as "name only" and fully private members, shown without revealing details
العربية · RTL
English · LTR
02

Digital family card

البطاقة الرقمية

A card people want to gift and screenshot. It carries the family ribbon, a gold-ringed portrait, a bilingual name, profession tags, one-tap contact and a QR code to save the contact.

  • Family ribbon that puts lineage first, which matters culturally
  • Bilingual name with English and Arabic set in matching type
  • One-tap contact by WhatsApp, phone or email, plus a QR code for in-person sharing
  • Share as image as the primary action, because cards spread through WhatsApp
  • Apple Wallet and copy link as secondary actions
العربية · RTL
English · LTR
03

Public wedding gift

هدية زفاف

A guest opens a shared link and can pay without logging in. The campaign is clear, amounts are quick to pick, local payment methods are supported, and the fee is shown openly.

  • No-login guest flow to remove the biggest drop-off point for one-off contributors
  • Preset amounts (200, 500, 1,000 or Other) to cut typing on mobile
  • Named or anonymous giving, which respects social norms around gifting
  • Apple Pay, mada and cards to match how people in Saudi Arabia actually pay
  • Transparent fee line and the exact amount that reaches the groom, to build trust
العربية · RTL
English · LTR
04

Family home

الرئيسية

A calm, welcoming starting point. The user gets a personal greeting, sees the active campaign first, and can reach the tree, fund, events, votes and directory from simple cards.

  • Personal greeting with family size ("482 members across 7 generations") to build belonging
  • Active campaign as the one dark, high-contrast card, with a single Contribute action
  • Four section cards each showing a live count, so the home screen doubles as a status view
  • Five-tab bottom nav reachable with one thumb
العربية · RTL
English · LTR
05

Family fund dashboard

صندوق العائلة

Who has paid and who is late, at a glance. It shows clear totals, each member's status, filters and an export option, with every amount set in tabular numerals.

  • Balance first with collected and overdue amounts shown separately
  • Paid and overdue counts as large numbers the treasurer can act on
  • Segmented filter for All, Paid and Overdue
  • Per-member rows with Arabic names and status, where overdue is shown in words and not only by color
  • Export for the family treasurer's records

Bilingual & RTL

Mirroring done properly

Mirrored, with exceptions

Layout, navigation order and progress bars flip for RTL. Phone numbers, QR codes and media controls don't.

Names in both scripts

Members carry Arabic and Latin names, shown together on cards and fund rows.

Local money formats

SAR currency, mada cards and Apple Pay, with consistent digit and separator rules in both languages.

Success metrics

How success would be measured

These are the metrics I set with the team to judge the design after launch:

Guest gift conversion

The share of people who open a gift link and complete payment, the main test of the no-login flow.

On-time fund payments

The overdue count per month, compared with the treasurer's spreadsheet baseline.

Tree completeness

Verified members per family, and how many generations are covered.

Card shares

Cards shared as images or added to Wallet, which signals organic growth.

Reflection

What I learned

  • Cultural fit is a usability feature. Lineage, gifting etiquette and privacy around women shaped the information architecture as much as user flows did.
  • Design RTL first, then mirror. Starting from Arabic avoided the cramped, translated feel that most bilingual apps have.
  • In money flows, trust comes from transparency. One clear line saying "reaches the groom in full" does more for trust than any badge.
