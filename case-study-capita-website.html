Redesigning Capita.com on Drupal for clients, careers and investors

Capita is a consulting, transformation and digital services business that serves millions of people across governments and businesses. I led the UX/UI redesign of Capita.com during its move to Drupal 10, rebuilding navigation around user problems and creating a responsive, accessible component library.

The challenge

Problem statement and pain points

The site had fragmented UI, an outdated technology stack and weak responsiveness, which hurt engagement, SEO and stakeholder satisfaction.

User experience

  • Navigation complexity: users struggled to find services across 200+ pages
  • Content fragmentation: duplicate, inconsistent messaging
  • Technical debt: legacy CMS limited design and content updates
  • Mobile: 60% of traffic on mobile, with a weak responsive design

Technical limitations

  • Drupal 8 → 10 migration underway, but legacy modules and themes still hurt performance and security
  • No central governance for UI/UX decisions across Drupal, SEO, content and design

SEO & content

  • Inconsistent heading tags and anchor-link behaviour
  • Missing hover states and semantic markup for accessibility and SEO

Responsiveness & accessibility

  • UI broke on mobile because of oversized spacing and elements
  • Missing alt text, keyboard navigation and contrast fixes

Seven goals for the redesign

A modern, accessible site that communicates Capita’s value to clients, candidates and investors, on a CMS editors can update quickly.

Approach

Solutions to the challenges

  1. Persona-based journeys: journey mapping and usability testing with real users.
  2. Responsive design: responsive frameworks plus automated accessibility testing with AXE and NVDA.
  3. High-fidelity validation: prototypes tested before development.
  4. Drupal 10 migration: standardised modules and themes for performance and security.
  5. Cloud-native architecture: aligned with Capita’s enterprise security principles.
  6. Cross-functional governance: UX, SEO, content and tech decide together.
  7. Agile delivery: sprints with regular stakeholder feedback loops.

Rationale

Design rationale and decisions

Four decisions that shaped the redesign, each backed by research.

Problem-based vs feature-based navigation

Challenge: Service names like “Customer Experience Management” didn’t match how users talk (“How do we reduce costs?”).

Evidence: 65% grouped by problem; 58% confused by current names.

Decision: Renamed navigation in user language, e.g. “Improve customer experience”.

Progressive disclosure for services

Challenge: 8 services + 9 industries = 40+ visible options, causing decision paralysis.

Evidence: 70% preferred simplified navigation.

Decision: 5–6 primary services by default; specialist services expandable.

Progressive forms over long forms

Challenge: 7–8 field forms caused about 70% abandonment.

Evidence: A/B benchmarks: progressive forms cut abandonment ~40%.

Decision: Three essential fields first; conditional fields appear as needed.

Role-based homepage segmentation

Challenge: Job seekers, B2B buyers and investors have very different goals.

Evidence: Card sorting revealed different mental models by role.

Decision: Role selector in the hero, with tailored content and CTAs.

Principles

Design laws applied

UX laws that informed decisions where research alone didn’t settle the choice.

Fewer choicesHick’s Law

Applied: Visible services reduced from 8 to 5–6.

Impact: Faster decisions.

Familiar modelsJakob’s Law

Applied: Problem-based language that matches how users think.

Impact: Less hesitation.

Limited memoryCognitive Load Theory

Applied: Progressive disclosure lowers information density.

Impact: Fewer form drop-offs.

GroupingGestalt principles

Applied: Services grouped by problem with consistent visual treatment.

Impact: Clearer hierarchy and navigation.

Distinct = memorableVon Restorff Effect

Applied: CTAs stand out through colour, size and position.

Impact: Higher CTA engagement.

5–7 itemsMiller’s Law

Applied: No more than about seven visible options at once.

Impact: Less cognitive friction.

Closing

Key takeaways

  • Low-code at enterprise scale: built on Acquia Drupal with Site Studio for modular themes and custom components.
  • Connected content: Workday powers live job listings, and REST APIs bring in external data.
  • One design system: Adobe XD and ZeroHeight keep components, colour, type and logo usage consistent, though the documentation still needs work.
  • New components: navigation blocks, homepage slider and banners, and better “Get in touch” and case study pages.
  • Shared ownership: UX, content strategists, writers and SEO engineers worked together for consistency and discoverability.
